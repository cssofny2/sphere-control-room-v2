# Trace the Sphere Claim Before Simulating

“Bashar sphere location test” is best understood as an informal label for a claim attributed to Bashar, as presented by Darryl Anka, and a later S.P.H.E.R.E. research proposal inspired by it—not the name of a validated experiment. The closest reported source is a third-party transcript of a 2013 *A Game of Chess* discussion describing a hollow copper sphere measured at two locations and then purportedly excited to move toward a destination-associated location; its original audio, exact wording, and passage timestamp have not been verified here. S.P.H.E.R.E.’s LOC-1 proposal narrows that story to a controlled A/B comparison of a selected resonance against a stationary reference; it does not test teleportation or establish a location-dependent physical effect. Project-authored documents describe a preregistration and apparatus plan, but the linked OSF record and Zenodo archive were inaccessible, and no experimental results or independent physical replication were found in the accessible sources. The current lab repository already contains a clearly synthetic LOC-1 teaching workspace, but its model values and provisional schema are not authoritative protocol specifications. This report prepares an evidence-labeled simulator path; **no test-specific code was implemented for this report**.

## The best-supported source is a transcript, not verified speech

The user’s phrase does not appear to be a formal source title. The most likely referent is the sphere discussion in a third-party transcript headed “Gravity and Vibrational Location,” part 2 of *A Game of Chess*, attributed to June 1, 2013. The inspected transcript describes comparing a thin hollow copper sphere at two ends of a flat table, then proposes that excitation associated with the second position could cause the sphere to roll toward it—or perhaps disappear and reappear. The transcript’s repository source is directly readable, but it establishes only what that third party published: the original recording, event metadata, speaker attribution, transcript accuracy, and the precise moment of the sphere passage were not authenticated ([2013 transcript source](https://github.com/jundalisay/superphysics/blob/148e0d6aff8491e1578dab9eb22eb0abf3ddb99f/content/en/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2013/2013-06-01b.md); [part 1 title source](https://github.com/jundalisay/superphysics/blob/148e0d6aff8491e1578dab9eb22eb0abf3ddb99f/content/en/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2013/2013-06-01.md)).

An earlier February 28, 1998-attributed third-party transcript is a separate lead, not proof of the claim’s origin. Its version describes measuring a conductive sphere in an isolated chamber and trying to induce destination-directed rolling acoustically; it gives different size/material details. A 2019-attributed transcript discusses a nickel-copper sphere and says the room, table, and excitation apparatus affect the response, while a 2023-attributed transcript revisits measurement and distinguishes rolling from disappearance/reappearance. These later or earlier accounts should not be collapsed into one stable protocol: details vary by transcript, and none was checked against original audio. The 1998 file’s linked video was not inspected; the 2019/2023 passages are also transcript evidence, not experimental reports ([1998 transcript source](https://github.com/jundalisay/superphysics/blob/148e0d6aff8491e1578dab9eb22eb0abf3ddb99f/content/en/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/1998/1998-02-28.md); [2019 transcript source](https://github.com/jundalisay/superphysics/blob/148e0d6aff8491e1578dab9eb22eb0abf3ddb99f/content/en/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2019/2019-06-22b.md); [2023 transcript source](https://github.com/jundalisay/superphysics/blob/148e0d6aff8491e1578dab9eb22eb0abf3ddb99f/content/en/research/other/alien/essassani/transmissions/2023/2023-09-10c.md)).

The official *A Game of Chess* and *Ringing the Bell* pages are useful title leads, but the original recordings could not be reached in this research; an official listing or a general Q&A chapter start does not verify the transcript or locate its sphere passage ([official *A Game of Chess* listing](https://tv.bashar.org/programs/game-of-chess); [official *Ringing the Bell* listing](https://tv.bashar.org/programs/ringing-the-bell)). A separate October 2026 provenance audit reports these access limitations and distinguishes transcript leads from authenticated audio; it is a secondary researcher’s audit, not an official transcript or experiment ([provenance audit](https://github.com/occult-kranti/brainwave_opensync/blob/336e3fe74e4513b314333b565455f0afa85eca1c/location-spectrum/research/bashar-provenance.md)). Accordingly, the careful attribution is “a claim attributed to Bashar, as presented by Darryl Anka,” not “Bashar demonstrated” or “the recording says” as an independently verified fact.

## LOC-1 operationalizes resonance comparison, not relocation

S.P.H.E.R.E.’s project-authored whitepaper says its hypothesis was inspired by Bashar material and frames it as a testable question, not physical evidence. The proposal asks whether moving one controlled copper test sphere between fixed A/B positions produces a repeatable selected-resonance difference relative to a stationary reference sphere, after controls and above a preregistered effect threshold. Its operational “location” is the A/B position label in one enclosure. That is a narrower and more measurable claim than destination-directed rolling or teleportation; a detected frequency contrast would not, by itself, demonstrate either motion or a novel mechanism ([project whitepaper v1.0 source](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper.mdx); [project protocol page source](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/protocol/index.html)).

The accessible v1.0 proposal describes A and B centers 0.40 m apart, one moved test sphere, and a stationary reference at a third position. Its public design defaults include at least 40 placements per position, ten blocks of eight balanced randomized placements, a nominal 30-minute settling time, and environmental logging at 1 Hz or faster. It proposes a 3D scanning laser Doppler vibrometer (LDV) for optical mechanical-motion readout, with a vector network analyzer’s S11 response as an exploratory/diagnostic channel, and a shared reference clock. Temperature, humidity, pressure, magnetic field, and vibration are among the logged conditions; sham moves, rotations, dummy-artifact checks, reference-only drift segments, and removal/replacement trials are described as controls. Instrument models, target thermal stability, and the roughly 9.2 mm artifact dimensions are proposal or provisional values—not evidence of purchased equipment, qualified apparatus, or a performed experiment ([project whitepaper v1.0](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper.mdx); [environment-replay proposal](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/research/environment-replay-copper-sphere-relocation.mdx)).

The proposal defines the main observable as a test/reference frequency ratio, but accessible drafts differ in how they normalize the A–B contrast. The site whitepaper states two-sided primary α = 0.01 and at least 90% power as planning goals, while saying the smallest effect of interest (SESOI) should be frozen after qualification. The separate v1.1 technical draft leaves SESOI, equivalence alpha, and a design-effect field null; it is explicitly not operationally adopted. The accessible text therefore does not justify inventing a ppb threshold, final analysis formula, or exact exclusions and stopping rule. It describes outcomes such as equivalence, detectable difference, candidate signal, and inconclusive, and states that a signal should prompt investigation and replication rather than a claim of new physics ([v1.0 whitepaper](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper.mdx); [v1.1 draft](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper-v1.1-full.md)).

Status claims require a qualification. The project’s protocol page says LOC-1 was registered on OSF and calls OSF the controlling preregistration, with the whitepaper serving as context; the project repository’s dated changes document that announcement and later shift in site wording. But the linked [OSF record](https://osf.io/5b6mj/overview) and [Zenodo whitepaper DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23583932) could not be accessed, and API attempts failed at DNS resolution rather than returning a meaningful record response. Thus registration identity, exact frozen variables, finalized thresholds, amendments, archived PDF contents, and whether the readable drafts match those records remain unverified. The four-object synchronized proposal is described by the project as an unadopted amendment, not the registered two-sphere design ([four-object proposal](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/protocol/synchronized-four-object-frequency-measurement/index.html); [project commit announcing registration](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/commit/726dd95fb836331007c50e9ecb9ab41a46196a4f); [later wording change](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/commit/1adc5466647d97f65277efbbaf7aca5283f7e61e)).

## Evidence supports a proposal, not a result

The source classes answer different questions and should remain distinct in the simulator:

| Source class | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Third-party attributed transcript | A publisher presents words and a proposed sphere scenario as an attributed Bashar/Anka exchange. | Original-audio fidelity, a measured outcome, or a validated physical mechanism. |
| S.P.H.E.R.E. project-authored protocol and whitepaper | The project’s own research question, proposed apparatus, design defaults, and claimed record status. | Independent confirmation, actual qualification, or completed data collection. |
| OSF/Zenodo links | The project points to these as the controlling registration and archived whitepaper. | Their contents or status in this research; neither record was successfully opened. |
| Independent modeling and accessible scientific context | A model can explore assumptions; conventional metrology provides relevant concepts. | Physical replication of this specific claim. |
| Physical experimental evidence | None identified in the accessible notes and sources. | A conclusion that nobody has ever attempted the experiment, or a positive/negative result. |

No controlled, independently documented physical replication, raw A/B measurement series, or peer-reviewed report testing this specific sphere claim was identified in the accessible sources. The independent Location Spectrum Lab project explicitly reports no physical sphere measurements, and S.P.H.E.R.E.’s protocol materials report no experimental results ([independent model README](https://github.com/occult-kranti/brainwave_opensync/blob/336e3fe74e4513b314333b565455f0afa85eca1c/location-spectrum/README.md); [project protocol page source](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/protocol/index.html)). This is a bounded search result: web search and several source/API endpoints were unavailable, so it is not proof that no one has ever tried. Nor does a proposed study, a transcript, or synthetic data count as an experimental result.

## Resonance physics sets guardrails for an educational model

In the conventional idealization of a homogeneous elastic sphere, an internal mechanical resonance is a mode of the object-and-boundary system, not a single universal “frequency of a place.” Its modes depend on dimensions, density, elastic properties, temperature, and boundary/support conditions; excitation and sensor placement determine which modes appear in a measurement. A pure coordinate translation of an isolated sphere in an otherwise unchanged uniform environment does not, within that ordinary model, change its intrinsic eigenfrequencies. A real A-to-B transfer does change the sphere’s relationship to fixtures, acoustic or electromagnetic excitation, air, temperature gradients, vibration, and readout geometry, so a positional measurement difference can arise conventionally. A frequency difference is therefore an observation to explain, not evidence by itself for an intrinsic location variable. Classical elastic-sphere mode theory is a useful next source to verify, but the cited Lamb paper was not directly retrieved during this work ([Lamb, “On the Vibrations of an Elastic Sphere,” DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s1-13.1.189); source access and bibliographic details were not verified in the research notes).

The often-repeated 230 kHz/230.5 kHz value needs especially careful labeling. In the project materials, “above 230 kHz” is a selection criterion; a thin-shell estimate around 230 kHz and a 9.2 mm diameter are proposal-stage design assumptions. The accessible attributed transcripts do not establish 230 kHz as an authenticated instruction from the original recording. Nor does the checked-out app’s 230,500 Hz baseline identify a measured copper breathing mode: its model is synthetic and has no elastic-sphere eigenmode solver. Copper-specific constants, artifact grade, actual geometry, mode roots, and mode-shape validation were not established here; the NIST OFHC copper data page is a source lead, not a value checked for this report ([project whitepaper v1.0](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper.mdx); [NIST OFHC copper data lead](https://trc.nist.gov/cryogenics/materials/OFHC%20Copper/OFHC_Copper_rev.htm); [app teaching model](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/simulation/model.ts)).

Temperature, support and contact stiffness, orientation, residual stress, excitation loading, mode identity, sensor alignment, clock behavior, and environmental vibration are relevant potential confounds. The project’s whitepaper gives a provisional thermal sensitivity estimate; the app separately uses +5 Hz/°C in its teaching model, while its protocol panel displays +23 Hz/K. These numbers conflict because they are modeling assumptions, not validated copper coefficients. For instance, 10 mK times 23 Hz/K is 0.23 Hz by arithmetic, but that is not an empirically supported prediction for a particular sphere. An S11 dip is also an electrical response of a coupled fixture, not automatically a confirmed mechanical eigenfrequency. Any teaching discussion of these issues should carry the “illustrative” qualification and avoid presenting simulated uncertainty as a complete measurement budget ([project whitepaper v1.0](https://github.com/cssofny2/spheredesci.org/blob/3a651256e92f5a674e34f44e8756f30cd1caf338/content/whitepaper.mdx); [synthetic measurement implementation](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/simulation/measurement.ts); [GUM publications, a metrology reference lead not checked here](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/jc/jcgm/publications)).

## The repository can host a labeled simulator, not a physical replica

The active app entry loads `MetrologyLabV3.tsx`; the README identifies `src/simulation/model.ts` and `measurement.ts` as a teaching resonance model and Lorentzian trace fitter. The model hard-codes a 230,500 Hz baseline and adjustable teaching position/temperature effects; its own source comment states simulator output is illustrative, not experimental evidence. Its fitted resonance and covariance uncertainty describe a synthetic S11 trace and noise-only fit uncertainty, not traceable hardware calibration or all systematic influences ([repository README](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/README.md); [active app entry](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/main.tsx); [teaching model](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/simulation/model.ts); [measurement fitter](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/simulation/measurement.ts)).

`src/protocol/ProtocolWorkspace.tsx` already provides a synthetic LOC-1 educational workspace, including a schematic 0.400 m A/B rail, provisional artifacts, illustrative uncertainty and statistical panels, and simulated controls/custody. It explicitly says it is not an approved preregistration, calibrated facility, or source of experimental evidence. The rail is a schematic independent of the legacy XYZ stage’s ±35 mm operating range; it should not be interpreted as a runnable physical A/B protocol. Its 25-field `SPHERE-LOC1-provisional` v0.1 export appropriately leaves unknown fields null, but its normalized contrast divides by a fixed 230,500 Hz rather than measuring the proposed test/reference ratio, so it must not be presented as an authoritative LOC-1 data contract ([protocol workspace](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/protocol/ProtocolWorkspace.tsx); [provisional schema and converter](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/protocol/loc1.ts); [run contract](https://github.com/cssofny2/sphere-control-room-v2/blob/main/src/domain/run.ts)).

Preparation should proceed in distinct stages. First, obtain and compare the actual OSF registration and Zenodo PDF against the project’s public drafts; confirm the adopted contrast, SESOI/equivalence bounds, exclusions, stopping rules, acquisition settings, and primary mode before treating any value as fixed. Second, preserve the current general teaching simulator but separate a null/no-position-effect baseline from explicit “hypothetical signal” and “conventional confound” scenarios. Include equivalent, detectable, confounded, technically uninterpretable, and inconclusive cases rather than making an anomalous shift the default outcome. Third, keep transcript provenance and experimental-plan provenance visible in source cards, and label all generated values in screens and exports “Synthetic educational data—not measurements or evidence for the claim.” Fourth, only after sourcing and numerical validation, add an ideal elastic-sphere model with explicit geometry, material, mode and support assumptions; do not label a generated 230.5 kHz trace as a breathing mode without mode calculation and mode-shape evidence. Finally, version any future export contract with explicit units, null semantics, data origin, protocol version, artifact and position identities, raw-data hashes, calibration metadata and exclusions; do not conflate legacy XYZ coordinates with the proposal’s A/B placements. These steps prepare an educational simulation; hardware control, secure independent blinding, validated acquisition, and physical safety remain separate engineering and governance work.

## Conclusion

The defensible simulator subject is not “whether Bashar’s sphere test works,” but how a narrowly defined A/B resonance experiment could distinguish null, conventional-confound, and hypothetical-effect scenarios while teaching source quality and metrology. The claim’s transmission provenance is weaker than its modern research proposal’s documentary specificity, and that proposal’s public summaries are more accessible than its claimed controlling registration. Until the original recording and registry/archive are verified and real data are made public, no interface should imply that LOC-1 has produced evidence. The report adds no experiment-specific implementation; it identifies the existing educational foundation and the evidence and specification gates that should precede integration work.
